# Asset plan, budget and integration

Inventory what the current slice needs before requesting assets. Record
semantic key, purpose, source/provenance, format, dimensions or scale,
transparency/pivot, animation states, expected lifetime and current status.
Keep missing, generated, inspected and integrated as distinct states.

## Spend on what matters to play

Prioritize visible, repeated and mechanically important assets. Reuse modular
pieces when repetition supports the world; retain unique landmarks when they
help orientation. A small complete representative set is preferable to many
uninspected variants that consume the budget without improving the slice.

Check the currently offered tool cost and output contract. Keep room for
integration and verification after generation. Do not promise free music,
unlimited retries, compression support or a model tool unavailable in this run.
Use an intentional procedural fallback when suitable; disclose a material art
limitation rather than inventing a finished result.

## Integrate real results

Retain exact returned keys and script tags. Register/load modules in the correct
order and handle asynchronous failure. Inspect sprite alpha, edge halos,
filtering and real frame grids. For models verify units, origin, grounding,
materials and actual clips from the gameplay camera.

Separate collider and visual ownership. A decorative mesh or transparent
padding should not define an unpredictable hitbox. Shared textures/materials
need shared lifetime; removing one actor must not break another.

Choose asset sizes from displayed detail and measured decode/upload/render
cost, not just the generator's maximum output. Load only what the active area
needs, bound queued work and retain useful placeholders. Do not hide a missing
critical mechanic behind an endless loading screen.

## Acceptance and provenance

Check the representative asset in context, two instances where applicable,
loading failure, scene exit during loading and repeated restart. Inspect actual
performance on the target device class. Keep source and licensing evidence for
provided/downloaded assets and generation receipts where available; a filename
is not proof of ownership rights.

Hand off an asset inventory with accepted keys, dependencies and unresolved
issues. Never claim that an attractive preview establishes a working game.
